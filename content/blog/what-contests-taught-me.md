I have taken part in contests organised at my university, including Unlock the Algorithm and Take Off, and I go to the hackathons the department runs. I am still a beginner. These are notes on what I have noticed so far.

## The result matters less than the review

After a contest, the useful part is going back over the problems I could not solve. I read the solution, close it, and write the code again without looking. If I cannot, I did not understand it.

## Solve the easy ones first

It is tempting to start with the interesting problem. The points are in the easy ones, and solving them carefully gives a base. Reading every problem first and sorting them by difficulty is a habit I have taken from watching how others work.

## Practice a little, often

An hour on most days did more for me than a long session once a week. Contest problems repeat a small number of ideas: sorting, graphs, searching, simple dynamic programming. Seeing the same idea in different clothes is how it becomes automatic.

## Hackathons are different

A hackathon is not about the cleverest algorithm. It is about choosing a small piece of the problem that you can finish, building it and explaining it. I found that explaining it clearly takes nearly as much work as building it.

## Other people

Talking to people who were better than me was the quickest way to improve. Most of them were glad to explain how they had approached a problem.

I do not have a long list of wins to show. What I have is a clear idea of what to work on next, and that seems to be most of what a contest is for.
