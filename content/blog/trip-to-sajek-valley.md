We went in the middle of January, which is the cold season, so the plan was
four friends, one rented microbus, and a night in a resort that had fewer
reviews than I would have liked. It turned out well, mostly because we left
early and because the walk to the viewpoint does not care about your schedule.

## Getting there

From Dhaka it is a long drive south, roughly six to seven hours depending on
how much of the Chittagong hill tract traffic you get stuck behind. The
practical notes:

- Leave Dhaka before 6am. The road is fine early and the last two hours before
  dark are the worst part, because the buses and trucks on the hairpin bends
  do not expect you.
- Fill the fuel tank in Cox's Bazar if you are coming from that side. There is
  one fuel stop that is expensive and one that is closed.
- A microbus with a driver who knows the road is worth the extra money. Ours
  had a phone charger and a second phone for the road, which saved the whole
  trip.
- Carry cash. Card machines work at the resort and nowhere else.

The last two hours are on a hill road with drop-offs that make you hold your
breath. If you can sit on the downhill side, sit there.

## The walk to the viewpoint

There is a viewpoint about an hour and a half walk from the resort, and it is
called a viewpoint for a reason. You go up through bamboo, then pine forest,
then past the king's bed, which is a rock formation with a story attached that
I will not repeat because I do not remember it.

Two things nobody tells you:

1. The path is easy going down and hard going up, in that order, because you
   walk it in the dark on the way back.
2. There is a tea stall about halfway with better tea than anything in Dhaka
   and a generator, which is the only place to charge a phone for about two
   kilometres.

We went up at four in the afternoon, took the photographs everyone takes, and
came back down at six. The light on the hills for about twenty minutes before
sunset is the reason to go at that hour rather than in the morning.

## The shortcut we should not have taken

Coming back, one of my friends insisted on cutting through a bamboo grove
because Google Maps drew a line that looked like a path. It is not a path. It
is bamboo, thick and ankle-deep, and we fought through it for forty minutes
with our bags catching on every stem.

> The line on the map is the shortest distance. The path is not always the
> shortest distance.

We came out at the main trail, filthy, thirty minutes later than planned, and
with a blister I still had a week later. The right move is to trust the trail
signs and accept the extra ten minutes.

## What it cost

For four of us, two nights, transport and food, it came to a bit under six
thousand taka each. The resort was not the cheapest, and we ate most dinners
in the tourist strip in Rangamati because the food is better and half the price
of the hotel restaurant.

Bring more warm layers than you think. January nights in the hills are cold
enough that you will be glad about the one jacket you did not expect to wear,
and the plastic bag your friend wrapped their phone in is a genuine lifehack
against humidity.

## Why I would go again

Not for the resort. For the hour of walking in the forest above the clouds, the
tea stall, and the fact that you can hear the road you drove up from the
viewpoint. It is close enough to Dhaka to do on a long weekend, which is the
only reason I keep going back.
